Overview: FPI mission

The mission of the Federal Program Inventory, or FPI for short, is to present on a single website — a coherent and comprehensive picture of all Federal programs and their associated program performance, spending, and improper payment rate information.

By providing a comprehensive catalogue of Federal programs, the FPI enhances transparency and promotes efficiency and accountability as a tool that will enable comparisons of like programs, identify duplicative activities across multiple programs, and ensure programs align with mission areas and key Administration priorities.

What data is used for the FPI?

The FPI relies on publicly available, agency-provided data in three authoritative systems: SAM.gov, USASpending.gov, and PaymentAccuracy.gov. For more information on the data used in the FPI, see the FPI Data, Terms, and Concepts page.

FPI Mandate and Background

The Government Performance and Results Act Modernization Act of 2010 (GPRAMA), as amended by the William M. (Mac) Thornberry National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2021 (NDAA), requires the Office of Management and Budget (OMB) to develop a single website that provides a "coherent picture of all Federal programs, and the performance of the Federal Government as well as individual agencies." This picture - known as the Federal Program Inventory, FPI for short - allows policymakers and the public to review and analyze Federal spending that shares a common purpose within and across agencies. An appropriately constructed catalogue of Federal programs can:

  • improve financial and program management through the establishment of stronger connections between spending and performance information,
  • reduce reporting burden,
  • curtail fraud, waste, and abuse by promoting accountability and enhanced transparency into programmatic information,
  • ensure programs align with mission areas and key Administration priorities, and
  • ensure Congress and OMB are better prepared to nimbly respond to emerging priorities.

FPI Timeline and Evolution

The Trump 47 Administration updated the FPI to include program performance, spending and improper payment information for the most recently completed fiscal year - FY 2026 - along with making several other key enhancements.

First, Assistance Listing programs were revised within the FPI in order to ensure a one-to-one relationship between financial assistance programs identified for improper payment reporting and programs in the FPI. In most instances, this update required agencies to break out large, uniform Assistance Listing into more natural, standalone and unique Assistance Listing programs as well as consolidate multiple programs that otherwise would have been grouped together into one single Assistance Listing. This enhancement served to increase program alignment by ensuring a 1:1:1 correspondence across Assistance Listing programs, the FPI, and PaymentAccuracy.gov reporting.

Second, to enable comparisons of like programs within and across agencies, a more systematic framework was introduced for organizing Federal program spending and performance information. Several new Categories to the existing list previously on the website were added, while a taxonomy of government-wide objectives and program outcomes has been created to better articulate in a more standardized, cross-government way what programs aim to achieve for the American people. With this enhancement, each program in the FPI is mapped to the government-wide objective and program outcomes to which they are best aligned.

Third, the types of programs represented in the FPI have been expanded beyond Federal Assistance Listings, tax expenditures, and interest on the public debt. With this enhancement, select acquisition programs (generally, a set of contracts that have been identified on USAspending.gov that helps the Federal government procure a product or service) are now captured in the FPI—adding over $123 billion in FY 2025 Federal spending not previously accounted for. This effort underscores OMB’s deliberate and iterative approach to achieve full implementation of the FPI and present a comprehensive picture of all Federal spending organized by program.

Finally, a number of enhancements were made throughout the website with this update, from expanded spending and performance information for programs where data was available, such as the integration of improper payment rate reporting from paymentaccuracy.gov, to improvements designed to enhance a user’s experience throughout the site.
