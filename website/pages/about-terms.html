Overview

This website leverages publicly accessible assistance listings (AL) data from SAM.gov and financial data from USAspending.gov, linked to assistance listings through the AL number, as well as tax expenditure and interest data from Treasury.gov and improper payment data from paymentaccuracy.gov. The categories used to categorize programs can be found in the Annual Publication of Assistance Listings Functional Index, as well as on Treasury's publication of Tax Expenditures. Agencies submit data for their programs directly to these sources.

Explanation of terms and concepts

Assistance Listing

Assistance listings are detailed public descriptions of Federal programs that provide Federal financial assistance such as grants, loans, scholarships, insurance, and other types of assistance awards. Every assistance award must be aligned to an assistance listing, and every assistance listing must be authorized by congressional statute. 2 CFR 200.203 identifies the Federal Assistance Listings (ALs) as program information produced by the executive branch of the Federal Government.”

Assistance Listings are created and maintained by the administering agency on SAM.gov and must be updated annually. As a program type, ALs are the core of the FPI.

Data source: SAM.gov

Assistance Type

Agencies identify the form in which the Federal government provides assistance to the award recipient, including contracts, grants, loans, insurance, and direct payment. An assistance listing may provide different types of assistance.

Data source: SAM.gov

Category (Sub-Category)

Programs in the FPI are organized into over 20 Categories. These Categories organize Federal programs into topics based on their major purpose, thematically capturing the core missions and policy areas across the Federal government (e.g., National Defense, Health, Transportation).

These are further broken down into program Sub-Categories, focus areas that provide for more specific groupings within each of the broader mission areas represented by the Categories.

Eligible applicant type

Agencies identify entities that are eligible to apply for each program when creating the assistance listing. The main purpose of this is to inform potential applicants of the programs for which they can apply. Information on award recipients can be found at USAspending.gov.

Additional specifics about eligible applicants for each program can be found on the program’s assistance listing on SAM.gov.

Data source: SAM.gov

Eligible beneficiary

Agencies specify who may receive the ultimate benefits from the program. Programs that provide direct assistance from a Federal agency will generally have the same applicant and beneficiary. In cases where assistance is provided through State and local governments, the applicants and beneficiaries may be different since the assistance is transmitted to private sector beneficiaries who do not have to request or apply for the benefits.

Additional specifics about eligible beneficiaries for each program can be found on the program’s assistance listing on SAM.gov.

Data source: SAM.gov

FPI Taxonomy

The FPI Taxonomy is a classification system designed to provide a more systematic framework for logically organizing Federal programs and the information associated with those programs. Components of the classification system include Categories, Sub-Categories, Government-wide Objectives, and Program Outcomes. The taxonomy makes it easier to find programs that contribute to common, shared Objectives and Outcomes while improving public understanding about what Federal programs currently operate, and how programs link to budget, performance, and other information. These linkages also provide important context for agency activities. Each program in the FPI is mapped to a Government- wide Objective, and up to three Program Outcome(s) to which they are best aligned.

Government-wide Objective

Government-wide Objectives are a component of the FPI taxonomy’s classification system and represent a standardized, consistent set of goals that cut across agencies and programs that the Federal government aims to achieve. Each program in the FPI is mapped to one Government-wide Objective to which it is best aligned.

Improper Payment

A payment that was made in an incorrect amount under statutory, contractual, administrative, or other legally applicable requirements. The term improper payment includes; any payment to an ineligible recipient; any payment for an ineligible good or service; any duplicate payment; any payment for a good or service not received, except for those payments where authorized by law; and any payment that does not authorized by law; and any payment that does not account for credit for applicable discounts.

Data source: Paymentaccuracy.gov

Improper Payment and Unknown Payment Rate

The degree of Improper Payments and Unknown Payments measured against the outlays.

Data source: Paymentaccuracy.gov

Obligation

Obligation means a binding agreement that will result in outlays, immediately or in the future. When awarding funding, the U.S. government enters a binding agreement called an obligation—which is essentially a promise to spend the money, either immediately or in the future. An agency incurs an obligation, for example, when it places an order, signs a contract, awards a grant, purchases a service, or takes other actions that require it to make a payment. Additional detail is provided in OMB Circular A-11.

Each year, agencies report actual obligations for the past fiscal year and estimated obligations for the current and budget fiscal years. Likewise, in accordance with 2014 DATA Act, agencies are required to report obligation information on a quarterly and/or monthly basis on USAspending.gov. Program obligations from USAspending.gov are calculated by taking the total of obligations aligned to the assistance listing at the transaction level for the fiscal year.

Note that the obligations information reported on SAM.gov may not match what is reported on USASpending.gov. Below is a non-exhaustive set of explanations that may explain differences between these data sources:

  1. Due to the timing of reporting in the two systems, data from one system may be more up-to-date than the other.
  2. USASpending.gov total obligations include all transactions that occur within a fiscal year, including de-obligations for awards made in prior fiscal years. SAM.gov data may or may not include these de-obligations.
  3. There are different ways to account for obligations made through loans or insurance. Agencies were not instructed on how to account for these obligations on SAM.gov, and may have chosen a different method than what is reported on USASpending.gov.

For the purposes of this inventory, the website displays estimated obligation from sam.gov for the most current fiscal year and the obligations information from USAspending.gov for all preceding years to arrive at the most accurate picture of the funding level for a given program.

Data sources: SAM.gov and USAspending.gov

Outlay

Outlay means a payment to liquidate an obligation (other than the repayment of debt principal or other disbursements that are "means of financing" transactions). Outlays generally are equal to cash disbursements but also are recorded for cash-equivalent transactions, such as the issuance of debentures to pay insurance claims, and in a few cases are recorded on an accrual basis such as interest on public issues of the public debt. In short, Outlays are the measure of Government spending that has occurred, and generally, will lag behind obligations as a matter of order of operations.

For the purposes of this inventory, the website displays outlay information from USAspending.gov for all years for a given program.

Data source: Paymentaccuracy.gov

Program

For the purposes of this inventory, a program is an individual assistance listing as submitted by agencies to SAM.gov, an individual tax expenditure as described by Treasury, or the sum of interest on the public debt. With the Spring, 2026 update, select acquisition programs are also now represented.

  • Federal Assistance Listings. Assistance listings are detailed public descriptions of Federal programs that provide Federal financial assistance such as grants, loans, scholarships, insurance, and other types of assistance awards.
  • Tax Expenditures. Tax expenditures are defined by law as “revenue losses attributable to provisions of the Federal tax laws which allow a special exclusion, exemption, or deduction from gross income or which provide a special credit, a preferential rate of tax, or a deferral of tax liability.” These exceptions may be viewed as alternatives to other policy instruments, such as spending or regulatory programs. For tax expenditure programs, amounts shown are the sum of revenue losses and outlays. Learn more at Treasury.gov.
  • Interest on the Public Debt. Interest expenses the government pays on its outstanding loans. Also referred to as the cost to the U.S. for borrowing money.
  • Acquisition Programs. Procurement of products or services in the form of a contract or a set of contracts. For example, when the Department of War utilizes a set of contracts to buy a weapon system, that procurement is considered an acquisition program. Amounts shown are the sum of obligations and the sum of outlays from a set of contracts in a given year, based on transaction action data on USAspending.gov. Because contract data is drawn directly from USAspending.gov and its rolling reporting cadence, current year amounts will lag behind preceding years. The obligation of the immediately preceding year will represent the most accurate funding level of the acquisition program.

Data sources: Sam.gov, USAspending.gov, Treasury.gov

Program Outcome

Program Outcomes are a component of the FPI taxonomy that help show how government spending translates, or links, the program’s purpose and objectives into measurable results being delivered. Because many programs are authorized and designed to deliver results that transcend more than one outcome, programs in the FPI can be mapped to between one and three Program Outcomes to which they are best aligned.

Unknown Payment

A payment that could be either proper or improper, but the agency is unable to discern whether the payment was proper or improper as a result of insufficient or lack of documentation.

Data source: Paymentaccuracy.gov
