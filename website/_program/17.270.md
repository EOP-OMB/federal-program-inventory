objective: 'The Reentry Employment Opportunities (REO) program encompasses two distinct
  grant categories: adult reentry grants and youth/young adult-focused grants. Adult
  reentry grants are designed to enhance workforce reintegration for individuals returning
  from incarceration by increasing employment opportunities, improving job retention,
  and raising earnings, while simultaneously reducing recidivism rates. Youth-focused
  grants address the needs of young individuals involved in or at risk of involvement
  in crime and violence. The objectives include preventing school dropout among in-school
  youth, improving literacy and numeracy skills, increasing employment rates for out-of-school
  youth, and mitigating both criminal activity and recidivism among youth populations.
  Collectively, the REO program seeks to facilitate successful community reintegration
  for justice-involved individuals through the development of evidence-based strategies
  and collaborative partnerships that promote sustainable workforce outcomes.'

- description: In Fiscal Year 2025, ETA anticipates distributing funds totaling $81,000,000
    to states and non profit organizations to support adults, young adults and youth
    with justice involvement with the advancement of workforce readiness skills. Goals
    for the grant funds will support in-demand industries and are aligned with the
    America's Talent Strategies. Prior to FY2025, ETA distributed grant funds totaling
    $92,561,555 to 31 public or private nonprofit organizations, community and faith-based
    organizations for the administration of the Pathway Home (adult) and Growth Opportunities
    (youth/young adult) Programs. The adult projects provide reentry programming to
    individuals prior to release from state correctional facilities and county and
    local jails with continued employment-focused services after release. The youth
    projects prepare justice-involved youth and young adults for employment through
    training activities and paid work experience, as well as incorporate community
    violence intervention. As of quarter ending 6/30/2025, 86% of adults received
    a credential and 50% were employed after exiting the project. Additionally, 70%
    of youth received a credential and 62% of youth were in education or employment
    after exiting the project.
